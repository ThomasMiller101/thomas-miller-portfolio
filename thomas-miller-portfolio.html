1. Introduction

Block copolymers can self-assemble into a variety of nanostructures, making them highly valuable for applications in various fields such as nanotechnology, materials science, and drug delivery. Understanding how to control their self-assembly and morphological transitions is crucial for designing these materials with tailored properties.

My specific role in the project was to determine the ideal simulation box size for the bulk period of the pattern to appear without interruptions or deformities to the expected pattern.

2. Methods

In this project, we use the automated workflow developed in our group to perform equilibrium molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of ABC mikto-arm star triblock terpolymers using a coarse-grained (i.e., the dissipative particle dynamics chain, DPDC) model [1], specifically for the purpose of finding the bulk period of the [8.8.4] tiling pattern.

The workflow uses an Excel worksheet to input the simulation parameters, performs simulations in parallel using HOOMD-blue, calculates various quantities (e.g., the mean-square chain radius of gyration, interaction energy, pressure tensor, and order parameter), plots the data, and finally summarizes important simulation results back into the worksheet. The bulk period is found when the difference among the diagonal elements of the pressure tensor as the change in D vanishes. [2]

3. Results

Each data point in the figure below corresponds to a simulation case with a different number of ABC stars (each having 8 A-segments, 8 B-segments, and 4 C-segments connected at a common joint segment) in the simulation box at constant segment number density, thus a different cubic simulation box length L, where s denotes the range of the non-bonded pair potential between segments. We see that the change in D vanishes around L/s=15.66; that is, the distance between two nearest red (A-) or blue (B-)cylinders in the bulk, L0, is 7.83s.

As the change in D vanishes, the periodic boundary conditions imposed on the simulation box are commensurate with the bulk periodicity of [8.8.4] and the diagonal elements of the pressure tensor are equal, meaning that the pressure is the same in all directions and the structure is not deformed. [2] This has not been done in previous simulations of [8.8.4].

4. Discussion

The primary goal of this project is to investigate the structures and thermodynamic properties of the [8.8.4] tiling patterns formed by ABC stars. We also compared simulation results with predictions from the polymer self-consistent field (SCF) theory for the same model system, which gives L0=8.50s thus overestimating it. This allowed us to unambiguously quantify the consequences of approximations used in the theory and gave us better insight into the self-assembled structures of ABC stars.

5. Conclusion
  • We have used an automated workflow for equilibrium molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of the DPDC model to study the [8.8.4] tiling pattern formed by ABC stars.
  • We have determined the bulk period of [8.8.4] tiling pattern.
  • We have found that the widely used SCF theory overestimates the bulk period of [8.8.4].
  • Similar work on the intriguing (3.3.4.3.4) tiling pattern, which has rarely been studied in the literature, is currently underway.
